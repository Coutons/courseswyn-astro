## Why take a course in Spanish at all?

Two reasons, and they are not the same reason.

**If you are a Spanish speaker**, the question barely needs asking: technical English is a real tax on learning. A concept you would absorb in four minutes in Spanish takes twenty in a language you are still translating in your head, and by minute twenty you have lost the concept rather than learned it. The Spanish-language catalogue exists because the demand is there — {n(spanishStudents)} enrolments across {spanishSet.length} courses is not a niche.

**If you are learning Spanish**, a technical course is the best kind of input you can find. Coursebook dialogue teaches you to order coffee; a web development course teaches you to follow forty minutes of continuous, purposeful explanation, which is roughly the skill you actually need. Research on language acquisition is consistent that comprehensible input on a subject you care about beats graded dialogues you do not — and caring about the subject is the whole trick.

The honest limit: this is practice, not tuition. Nobody learns Spanish for the first time from an Angular course. If you are below about B1, take a language course first and come back; from B1 upward, switching one technical subject to Spanish is the fastest way to find out how much you really understand.

### Are courses taught in Spanish good for learning the language?

**They are good practice and poor tuition.** Following forty minutes of technical Spanish trains listening and vocabulary on material you care about, which is exactly why it works as input. It will not teach you Spanish from nothing, though — take a language course to about B1, then switch a technical subject over to Spanish.
